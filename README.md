## Core idea

Asymmetric Human–AI Agency was created to make one structural distinction explicit:

> **Capability may be delegated. Authority must not be silently inherited or generated by the AI system.**

AI may generate, simulate, summarize, recommend, evaluate, and—under later bounded architectures—even execute actions. None of those capabilities, by themselves, create organizational, legal, or operational authority.

The original January 2026 documents used stronger formulations such as “only humans may decide or commit.” Those documents are preserved as part of the design history. The later Responsibility Pathway work refines that position by separating **authority, capability, evidence, execution, external effect, recovery, assurance, and human return**.

---

## Why this repository existed

The original concern was **silent autonomy drift**: workflows in which AI moves from generating or recommending to effectively determining outcomes without an explicit responsibility-bearing transition.

AHAA treated this as an architecture problem rather than only a policy or model-behavior problem.

Early design themes included:

- capability vs. authority;
- explicit responsibility pathways;
- human veto and return points;
- prevention of silent authority expansion;
- visible confirmation and review boundaries;
- failure modes where AI output silently hardens into decision or action.

The accompanying **Safety-LCA** documents were an early attempt to turn those principles into a reference architecture.

---

## What still carries forward

Several ideas from AHAA remain foundational in the later work:

- **Authority ≠ capability**
- AI output ≠ authorized organizational decision
- evidence sufficiency ≠ authority
- execution receipt ≠ verified external effect
- recovery ≠ automatic restoration of authority
- Human Return should remain explicit
- Residual Owner should remain explicit
- technical systems should fail visibly rather than silently invent permission

---

## What changed after January 2026

The later Responsibility Pathway architecture became more precise than the original AHAA/Safety-LCA wording.

In particular, the current work does **not** require the simplistic rule that every consequential action must always end in an immediate manual human click. Instead it models bounded delegation, explicit authority, executable controls, Human Gates, evidence continuity, external-effect verification, interruption, repair, reconciliation, resume, and return to humans or institutions when required.

Likewise, AI or automated components may be treated as operational actors in a bounded system without being treated as sovereign sources of authority or final responsibility.

For the detailed comparison, see [Current Position and Lineage](./docs/07-Current-Position-and-Lineage.md).

## Relationship to the current Responsibility Pathway work

AHAA now sits upstream as **foundational conceptual lineage**.

The later work is separated into more specific layers:

- **Responsibility Pathway Model (RPM)** — analysis and diagnosis
- **[Responsibility Pathway Design (RPD)](https://github.com/YutoriKomeiji/responsibility-pathway-design)** — reviewable design translation
- **[Responsibility Pathway Engineering (RPE)](https://github.com/YutoriKomeiji/responsibility-pathway-engineering)** — bounded executable controls and engineering artifacts
- **[Responsibility Pathway Runtime (RPR)](https://github.com/YutoriKomeiji/responsibility-pathway-runtime)** — runtime authority/effect/recovery continuity
- **[Responsibility Pathway Operating System (RPOS)](https://github.com/YutoriKomeiji/responsibility-pathway-os)** — executable responsibility-state orchestration and operational continuity

The original documents in this repository remain useful for understanding how the later distinctions emerged, but they should not be cited as if they were the latest specification.

## Scope boundary

AHAA is not a model, SDK, product, safety proof, legal doctrine, certification framework, or production authorization mechanism.

Its current value is as a **design-origin artifact** documenting an early structural claim: increasing AI capability must not silently create or widen authority.
