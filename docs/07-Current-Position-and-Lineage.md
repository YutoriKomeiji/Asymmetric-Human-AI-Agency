# Current Position and Lineage — 2026 Review

## Repository origin

This repository was created on **2026-01-25 16:31:42 UTC** (**2026-01-26 01:31:42 JST**).

It captures an early design stage in which **Asymmetric Human–AI Agency (AHAA)** and **Safety-LCA** were used to state a structural separation between AI capability and human authority.

The enduring core proposition is:

> **Capability may be delegated. Authority must not be silently inherited or generated by the AI system.**

The original wording was intentionally strong because the immediate design problem was silent autonomy drift and human responsibility disappearing behind AI-enabled workflows.

## What remains foundational

The following ideas remain part of the conceptual lineage of the later Responsibility Pathway work:

- capability and authority must remain distinct;
- AI output is not, by itself, an authorized organizational decision;
- human return points must remain explicit;
- responsibility-bearing transitions should be visible and reviewable;
- convenience must not silently widen authority;
- external effects, interruption, recovery, and residual impact require explicit ownership.

## What has been narrowed or refined

Several statements in the January 2026 documents are broader than the later Responsibility Pathway architecture.

Examples include categorical formulations such as:

- only humans may commit;
- every irreversible action must always require a final human click;
- responsibility belongs to one specific human at every moment;
- AI is only a tool and never an actor;
- responsibility pathways must always terminate immediately at a human decision point.

The later work separates these concepts more carefully.

In the current Responsibility Pathway family:

- **Authority is distinct from execution capability.** An AI or automated component may execute a bounded action without thereby possessing the authority that justified it.
- **Evidence sufficiency is distinct from authority.** Satisfying stated evidence requirements does not create permission.
- **Execution receipt is distinct from verified external effect.** Completion may require readback or reconciliation.
- **Recovery does not regenerate authority.** Retry, resume, replan, repair, and reauthorization are distinct states.
- **Human Return and Residual Owner remain explicit**, but responsibility can be represented through roles, institutions, delegated scopes, operational gates, and persistent state rather than only a single immediate human confirmation step.
- Formal proofs, schemas, logs, and assurance records do not create legal, organizational, or operational authority.

## Relationship to later repositories

AHAA should now be read as **foundational design lineage**, not as the current canonical specification for the Responsibility Pathway architecture.

The later public work is separated into more precise layers:

- **Responsibility Pathway Model (RPM)** — analysis and diagnosis of responsibility-pathway structure and failure;
- **Responsibility Pathway Design (RPD)** — translation from diagnosed problems and authorized normative inputs into reviewable design;
- **Responsibility Pathway Engineering (RPE)** — bounded executable controls and engineering artifacts;
- **Responsibility Pathway Runtime (RPR)** — runtime continuity around authority declarations, Human Gates, execution attempts, readback, reconciliation, repair, and resume.

## Status

**Status: Foundational / historical design principle, retained for conceptual genealogy.**

The January 2026 documents remain useful as evidence of the earlier design stage and should not be silently rewritten to look as if they were authored with the later terminology already in place.

Where an older categorical statement conflicts with a later, more carefully bounded Responsibility Pathway distinction, the later specification should be treated as the current design position.
